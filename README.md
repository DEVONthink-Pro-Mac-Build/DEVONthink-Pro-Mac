## Nine moves from empty window to working archive

**1. Make the database about the deliverable.** Name it after the report, the thesis chapter or the
case — never after a year or a broad subject. A container with an end date stays comprehensible;
one called Research never does.

**2. Decide the storage question once.** Material can be copied inside the database file or indexed
where it already lives. Copy anything you would rather stop tracking manually; index folders that
other applications still open directly. Then leave the decision alone.

**3. Open a single landing spot.** Route everything through one inbox at first. Sorting during
capture breaks concentration, and a full inbox is a five-minute job on Friday rather than a
permanent tax on Tuesday.

**4. Capture the web while you are still reading it.** Send pages in as you find them so the source
address, publication date and title come along automatically. A link saved in a browser dies with
the site; a captured page does not.

**5. Feed the scanner and the phone into the same place.** Photographed pages and scanned paperwork
belong beside the digital sources. Confirm the text has been recognised, because an image of a page
is invisible to search until its words exist as words.

**6. Build three or four groups, not a hierarchy.** Shallow structures survive a growing project.
Depth feels organised for a fortnight and then becomes a maze you navigate by guesswork.

**7. Run one classification pass over the backlog.** Select the unsorted material, accept the
obvious proposals, reject the rest. Twenty minutes of this teaches the database more about your
categories than any amount of planning.

**8. Add tags on a second axis.** Groups say what a thing is; tags say what it is for — chapter,
argument, jurisdiction, interviewee. One axis, held consistently for a week, is worth more than five
invented on the spot.

**9. Interrogate it before you trust it.** Search a phrase you know is buried in a scan, join two
terms, exclude a third. If something refuses to appear, you have found a gap now instead of the
night before a deadline.

## Once the nine are done

Enable encrypted sync when the project has to be readable on an iPad or a phone, and hand the routine
collecting over to smart groups so new arrivals gather themselves against a rule rather than your
attention. The assistant is worth learning properly at this point: condense a forty-page filing to
decide whether it earns a full read, put a plain question to the material and verify the reply
against the source document, and open the suggested connections whenever an argument feels thin.
Annotate while reading, because highlights kept with the document remain searchable and are the
difference between an archive and a heap of saved files. If colleagues are involved, share the
database itself so they join the work instead of duplicating it.

## Small things that pay off in month two

Empty the landing spot every Friday; a hundred stray items are a coffee break, three thousand are a
project nobody starts. Retitle captured pages while you still remember why they mattered, since a
headline chosen by somebody else seldom explains your interest in it. Maintain a single index note
per project, holding links to whatever you reach for most, and open the database through that note.
And do not attempt to reorganise a decade of files in week one — that ambition, rather than the
software, is what strands most people who download DEVONthink Pro for Mac.
